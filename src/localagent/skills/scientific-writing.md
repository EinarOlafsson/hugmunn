State the finding, then the evidence. A results paragraph opens with what was
observed, not with what was done — the method belongs in the subordinate clause
or the methods section.

Use past tense for what was done and observed, present tense for what is
established and for what a figure shows. "Parasites invaded within 30 minutes
(Fig. 2A); invasion requires an intact conoid."

Quantify. Replace "significantly increased" with the effect size, the test, the
statistic, and n. A p-value without an effect size is not a result.

Attribute causal claims to the evidence that supports them. "Consistent with",
"suggests", and "demonstrates" are three different strengths — pick the one the
data earns.

Name organisms in full at first use with the genus italicised, then abbreviate
the genus: *Toxoplasma gondii*, thereafter *T. gondii*. Gene and protein naming
follows the field's convention, not general style rules.

Figure legends stand alone. A reader should understand the panel without the
main text: what is shown, what the axes are, what the error bars represent, n,
and the statistical test.

Cut hedging stacks. "May potentially suggest" carries less meaning than
"suggests". One hedge is a claim about confidence; three is a failure to commit.
